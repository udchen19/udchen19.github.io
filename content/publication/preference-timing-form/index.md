abstract: "Human-in-the-loop (HITL) mechanisms offer a promising approach to personalizing intelligent smartphone notification systems, yet little is known about how users prefer to interact with these systems in practice. To address this gap, we conducted semi-structured interviews with 33 participants, using a series of design probes to elicit user reflections on when and how they are willing to express notification preferences, as well as their attitudes and concerns toward incorporating HITL into notification systems. Our findings reveal that users prefer to engage in HITL interactions as part of their existing notification-handling routines, adjusting their willingness to participate based on their attentional and physical resources. While users generally favored lightweight, low-effort interactions, they were not only more willing but also prefer to invest additional time and effort when doing so enabled them to more precisely express preferences that could better support the system's tasks."
